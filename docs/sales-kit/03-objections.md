# Objection handling — the cold-lead playbook

You're calling people who enquired with us 1-6 months ago and never
booked. They're not hostile, but they're cooler than someone calling
in fresh. Goal of each call: book a slot OR get a clear "no thanks"
so we stop chasing them.

## The 30-second opener

> "Hi, is that {first_name}? — this is {your_name} calling from
> Clean Car Collective. You got a quote from us a {weeks_ago} weeks
> ago for a detail on your {vehicle}. I wanted to check in — was the
> timing just off, or did you sort something else? Either way I won't
> hold you up."

The "I won't hold you up" is the magic line. Gives them an out;
makes you sound like a person, not a script.

**If they're driving / busy**: "No worries, when's a better time to
catch you — I'll call back."

## The big objections (and what to say)

### "I forgot all about it / been busy"

Most common. They're warm, just need a nudge.

> "Yeah, life gets in the way. The car still need a sort, or has
> someone else done it?"

If yes → "Sweet, want me to lock in a time this week? I've got
Thursday afternoon or Saturday morning."

### "I went somewhere else"

That's a useful "no" — flip them to lost so we stop chasing.

> "All good — were you happy with the result? If not, give us a
> shout next time, we'd love to win you back. Otherwise I'll close
> this off so we don't keep nudging you."

**Mark them as `lost` in the CRM. Note who they went with if they
mention it.**

### "Price was too high"

This one matters — listen to whether it's "too high vs. competitors"
or "too high for what I can spend right now".

**Too high vs competitors**:
> "Fair enough — what were the other quotes coming in at? A lot
> depends on what's included. The {their_service} we quoted includes
> {2-3 key features from packages doc}. If you're comparing to a
> $150 hand wash, that's a different service."

If they had a genuinely comparable lower quote, offer the 5%
booked-today discount.

**Can't afford right now**:
> "All good. Did you want me to flag it on my calendar to check
> back in 4-6 weeks? Or is it more a 'try us next year' kind of
> thing?" — then mark callback in the CRM.

### "I don't think the car needs that much"

Downsell, don't lose them.

> "Yeah, fair point — if you've kept it pretty tidy, the Deluxe
> might be enough. That's $370 for a sedan — exterior wash, interior
> vacuum, plastics, windows, three-month sealant. Save the Premium
> for once-a-year. Want me to book the Deluxe in?"

### "How do I know it'll be worth it?"

Trust play.

> "Totally get it. We're {Christchurch / Wellington} based, we've
> been at it for {X} years, we've got Google reviews you can check —
> {if they ask}. We do a free 15-minute consultation if you want to
> meet the team before committing — no obligation, just have a look."

### "I'm going to do it myself / get a friend to do it"

Don't fight this — most people who say it actually will, but they
might not stick to it.

> "All good. If it turns into more of a job than expected, you've
> got my number. Want me to leave the file open or close it off?"

### "Just send me a quote, I'll think about it"

You already sent them one (it's why we're calling). Get specific.

> "We sent you one a few weeks back — was something missing from it,
> or was it more that the timing was off? Happy to walk through it
> while I've got you."

If they want a fresh quote → take their requirements again, quote on
the call, book the slot.

### "Do you cover [different suburb / region]?"

- **Christchurch shop**: covers all of Christchurch + Selwyn +
  Waimakariri. Anywhere else, transfer to in-store.
- **Wellington shop**: covers Wellington City, Lower Hutt, Porirua.
  Upper Hutt + Kapiti = in-store only.

Mobile pricing same as in-store. Confirm parking + power before
booking mobile.

## Closing — make the booking on the call

Once they sound interested:

> "Sweet. What works best — this week, next week, or further out?
> [check calendar / suggest 2 slots] Got Thursday at 9am or Saturday
> 11am, which is better for you?"

Always offer **two specific times**, not "what works for you?". Two
choices = decision; one open question = stall.

Once they pick → use the **"Book this customer →"** button on their
profile page in the CRM. The form pre-fills with everything you've
already got. Confirm the details out loud as you fill them in.

## Closing — when they say no

That's also fine. Mark the lead as **lost** in the CRM, leave a
short note ("price / went elsewhere / not interested"), move on.
You free up the system from chasing them and you get a clean call
list.

## Things you should never say

- "Just a quick call" (then talk for 10 minutes)
- "We're having a sale this week" (we're not — unless we are, ask
  Max/Ben first)
- "Best price you'll find anywhere" (we're priced fairly, not
  cheapest — that's the position)
- Anything you wouldn't want repeated back to you in a Google review

## When to escalate to Max or Ben

- Refund / complaint
- Discount > 5%
- Service we don't quote on the phone (ceramic, paint correction)
- Customer asking technical questions about products / chemicals
- They want to talk to "the owner"
